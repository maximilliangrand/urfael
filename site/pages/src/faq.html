Every question, answered

Honest answers about the Urfael assistant: cost, security, the Anthropic dependency, models, and how it proves what it did. Questions about {{ROBOT}} are answered on the {{ROBOT}} page. Still stuck? Ask on GitHub.

Is this just a wrapper around Claude?
The brain is the Claude you already pay for, reached through the claude CLI you are already signed into. Urfael is everything around that brain a wrapper is not: a tamper-evident ledger of every action, an ed25519 seal over it, a read-only sandbox for untrusted messages, a credential-deny boundary, nineteen chat channels (five of them exercised against live accounts), voice in and out, proactive active recall over your own notes, an experimental multi-agent Council, and a security benchmark you can run in one command. The model is rented. The sovereignty, the memory, and the safety are yours, on your machine, under MIT.
What does it cost to run?
By default, your existing Claude subscription, flat-rate within its plan limits and terms. There is no Urfael account and no server you rent from us. You can also point it at any of 30 providers (you pay their per-token rate) or at a local model on your own GPU, where it costs you only electricity. We do not see your traffic; there is nothing to see. On the subscription path, see the next answer about Anthropic's terms.
Does Urfael depend on Anthropic, and what if their terms change?
Urfael is not built, endorsed, or operated by Anthropic, and running it on a flat-rate Claude subscription depends on Anthropic's consumer terms, which we do not control. We say so plainly. It is not a single point of failure: the same Urfael, with the same permission profiles, also runs on any of 30 providers through a documented proxy, or on a local model on your own GPU, with one config line. So if those terms ever change, your cost model changes, not the product.
Is it actually secure, or is that just a word?
It is a command: npm run security. It boots the real daemon and dashboard and attacks them in ways modelled on attacks reported against self-hosted agents in 2026, then prints a pass-or-fail table. Its source defines 11 attack-class groups and 128 checks. Those are source counts, not a verified passing result, so run it and keep the output. You do not take our word for it. You run it.
Is Urfael an alternative to OpenClaw or Hermes Agent?
Yes, and an honest one. OpenClaw and Hermes Agent optimize for channel count and model breadth, and they are genuinely good at it. Urfael optimizes for what matters when a machine lives on your desk and acts for you: the smallest blast radius, a flat bill within your plan’s limits, local voice, and not overstating what it does. Where the others default to optional sandboxes and an inbound surface, Urfael’s daemon opens no network port (the optional dashboard, API and webhooks listen on loopback only), allowlists every sender before the brain sees a token, and runs remote turns read-only by default. It ships a security benchmark you run yourself (11 groups and 128 checks in the source) and a tamper-evident ledger so it can prove what it did. The full comparison, every win and every gap in the same table, is in the full comparison.
What is the most secure self-hosted AI agent?
Security is something you should be able to verify, not a word you read. Urfael was built blast-radius-first: no network port on the daemon (a 0600 unix socket; optional services listen on loopback only), fail-closed allowlists on every channel, read-only sandboxed remote turns, and autonomous work that can run in a Docker container with no secrets mounted, when you select Docker. The proof is a command, npm run security, which boots the real daemon and runs attack scenarios modelled on incidents reported against self-hosted agents in 2026, and which defines 11 groups and 128 checks in its source. Run it yourself rather than take anyone's word for it.
A prompt injection got other agents to leak secrets. Why not this one?
Because the containment is structural, not a clever prompt. Remote and untrusted turns run a read-only profile: read and search your notes, no shell, no write, and critically no network-egress tool. The vault also denies the agent reading your credential stores outright, a hard boundary that holds even in Full mode. So an injected "read a secret and send it somewhere" can't read your credential stores and has no web tool to send with. Tool limits don't guarantee the model ignores an injection, so treat untrusted content with care.
So is there any mode where an injection could leak something?
Yes, and we will not hide it. The default Fortress posture gives remote turns no web or shell tool, so untrusted content has no tool to send anything out with. If you deliberately enable Full mode the agent can fetch the web, which means a successful injection could exfiltrate notes you have given it, though never your credentials, because the credential-deny boundary still holds. Run Full mode only in a VM, a container, or a throwaway account. The benchmark, the docs, and this answer all say the same thing on purpose.
Why is there no inbound port? Doesn't that limit it?
It is the whole point. The brain listens on a 0600 unix socket only, never a TCP port. The topology is one-way: Urfael reaches out (to your Claude login, to chat APIs it polls); nothing reaches in. There is no gateway to expose, no token to leak over a socket, no DM endpoint to spray. A gateway that can be reached can be attacked; the daemon has none to reach. Optional services such as the dashboard, API and webhook receivers open loopback-only listeners when you turn them on, and a tunnel you set up can expose them.
Can I use a model other than Claude?
Yes. Claude is native through your subscription, the one path billed to you with no per-token meter (within your plan’s limits). Every other provider (OpenAI, Gemini, Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek, Groq, Ollama, LM Studio, NVIDIA NIM, OpenRouter, Bedrock, Vertex, Azure) runs through a documented Anthropic-compatible proxy such as claude-code-router or LiteLLM, with the same sandbox harness enforcing the boundaries. You can also switch models mid-conversation by just saying so: switch to opus.
Can it run fully offline, with nothing leaving my machine?
Yes. Point it at a local model (Ollama or NVIDIA NIM) and use the local voice model, and nothing leaves the machine. See the Local-GPU guide. You trade some capability for total air-gap; that is your call to make, not ours.
Can it prove what it actually did?
That is the trust suite, and it is real. Every action lands in a hash-chained Ledger of Record, so tampering is detectable. An ed25519 Sovereign Seal signs the chain head, so you can prove the record's authorship and integrity at a moment in time (it proves the record is authentic, not that every claim inside is true, we are precise about that). urfael why pickaxes the provenance of any lesson it learned. And urfael forget writes a tombstone for provable, consented deletion.
How mature is this, honestly?
macOS on Apple Silicon or Intel is the primary, best-tested target. Linux runs the full stack but has far less mileage. The Matrix, Signal and WhatsApp bridges, the QQ, SimpleX and PSTN phone bridges, and the eight native webhook channels are code-complete and reviewed, with their parsing and allowlist logic unit-tested and frozen as benchmark checks, but not yet battle-hardened against live accounts. The certified core, exercised against real accounts, is Telegram, Discord, Slack, iMessage and Email. Real-world scale is small, because this is a personal tool and we say so. There is a unit-test suite (its runtime summary gives the current total), an end-to-end harness against a live daemon, and adversarial security regressions frozen from real findings. What only time and users add is the one thing we cannot fake.
What is the Council?
A multi-agent orchestration you can watch. Urfael decomposes a hard question, dispatches read-only sandboxed workers to gather what each needs, then synthesizes one answer. The workers cannot write, cannot run a shell, and cannot reach the network; they read and report. You see the whole thing happen rather than trusting a black box.
