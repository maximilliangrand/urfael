Why {{ROBOT}}

Care is some of the most important work there is, and much of it happens at home, in small moments: a reminder, a glass of water, a chat on a quiet afternoon. The people doing that work are often stretched thin. The people receiving it can spend long hours on their own.

We don’t think a robot should replace any of those people. We think a kind, unhurried helper could take a little weight off their shoulders, and make the quiet hours a little less quiet.

What we want {{ROBOT}} to be

These are goals, not features. They’ll change as we learn.

  • {{icon:good-company}}

    Good company.

    Something to say good morning to. {{ROBOT}} should listen, chat and remember the things you’ve chosen to tell it.

  • {{icon:gentle-nudge}}

    A gentle nudge.

    Friendly reminders for the routines you set yourself: a walk, a phone call, a glass of water.

  • {{icon:bridge-to-your-people}}

    A bridge to your people.

    An easy way to reach family, friends or a carer when you’d like to.

  • {{icon:soft-presence}}

    A soft presence.

    Rounded, light and quiet. Made to be easy to be around, not to look like a machine.

  • {{icon:on-your-side}}

    Your helper, not ours.

    {{ROBOT}} should work for the people it lives with, just like our assistant does.

The promises we’re designing around

  • {{icon:gentle}}

    Gentle by design.

    Soft materials, slow movements, a calm voice. {{ROBOT}} should never startle, rush or pressure anyone.

  • {{icon:private}}

    Private by default.

    What happens at home should stay as private as possible. We’re designing {{ROBOT}} to keep as much as it can on the device, to ask before it shares anything, and to make its settings easy to read.

  • {{icon:honest-about-limits}}

    Honest about limits.

    {{ROBOT}} will say when it doesn’t know or can’t help. It won’t pretend to be a doctor, a nurse or a person.

  • {{icon:human-in-the-loop}}

    A human in the loop.

    Important decisions stay with people. {{ROBOT}} is meant to support the people who give care, never to stand in for them.

What {{ROBOT}} won’t do

  • It won’t diagnose, treat or give medical advice.
  • It won’t replace doctors, nurses, carers or family.
  • It won’t make decisions about anyone’s health or care.
  • It won’t listen in secret. When {{ROBOT}} is listening, you’ll be able to see it.
  • It won’t pretend to be a person.
  • It won’t share what it sees or hears without permission.
{{icon:call}}

{{ROBOT}} is not an emergency service. In an emergency, call 112 in Europe, or your local emergency number.

Where we are

{{icon:research}}

{{ROBOT}} is at the research stage. There’s no product to buy, no release date and no specification yet. Right now we’re working through the questions that come first:

  • What would make a helper like this welcome at home, and what would make it unwelcome?
  • How should it look, move and sound so that it feels calm and kind?
  • How can it be useful while keeping a home’s private life private?
  • Where are the lines it must never cross?

When there’s real progress to show, it will appear here first. Until then, we’d rather show you our questions than make you promises.

Questions about {{ROBOT}}

Can I buy {{ROBOT}}?

Not yet. {{ROBOT}} is a research project, and there’s nothing to buy or pre-order.

When will it be ready?

We don’t know yet, and we won’t guess. We’ll share real progress here when there is some.

Is {{ROBOT}} a medical device?

No. {{ROBOT}} is not a medical device. It isn’t designed to diagnose, treat, monitor or prevent any disease or condition.

Is this what {{ROBOT}} will look like?

The drawings here are concept art. {{ROBOT}}’s look will change as we design and test it.

Why the name?

{{ROBOT}} comes from ᚢ Uruz, the rune in our mark, often read as strength, health and vitality. It’s a working name for now.
